Running my life like a small company

KASCORP is a set of AI agents organized like a company, with one human employee. Here is how it is structured, the rules that keep it safe, and what building it has taught me.

By Gyewava Jonathan Kasule · Fordham MSBA

Like a lot of graduate students, I carry more than one life at once: coursework, work, a few personal businesses and a handful of projects I can't stop starting. For a while I handled it the usual way, with more apps, more tabs and more reminders. At some point I asked a different question. What would it look like if I ran all of this the way a small, well-run organization runs?

KASCORP is my answer. It is a set of AI agents organized like a company, and I am its only human employee.

The cast

At the front sits the Chief of Staff. It is the only one I talk to. I describe what I need in plain language, and it decides who should handle it, hands the work to the right department and comes back with one answer. I never have to remember which tool does what. That is the Chief of Staff's job.

Behind it are the departments, each with a clear remit. Growth looks after relationships and opportunities: the people I meet, the conversations worth continuing and the writing I put into the world. Academy handles learning, from coursework to the projects that come out of it. The Business desk keeps an eye on my personal businesses, their numbers and their strategy.

And then there is HR, my favorite. HR doesn't manage me. It manages the AI. When I want to add a new tool, HR interviews it first: what it can reach, whether it is safe, and whether something we already have could do the job instead. It recently hired Piper, an offline voice that reads replies aloud, after checking every file it downloaded. HR also reviews how the other departments perform and audits their conduct.

Two more members keep everyone honest. The Reviewer reads anything before it leaves the building and flags what should stay private. The Brand Library holds who I am, how I sound and what I never say in public, so every department speaks with one voice.

The rules of the house

The characters are the fun part. The rules are what make it work.

  • Read before you write. Nothing changes unless it needs to.
  • Agents draft, but never send.
  • A human approves everything that goes out.
  • Private things stay private, checked automatically before anything leaves.
  • Anything refused is stopped and logged.
  • New tools are interviewed before they are hired.

When an agent tries something it shouldn't, it is stopped and the attempt is logged, so I can see what was tried and why. None of these rules depend on the AI behaving well. They are built around it.

What building it taught me

Building this taught me more than I expected. The first lesson was that guardrails are design decisions, not afterthoughts. The most important choices I made were about what the system cannot do. The second was that the interesting work isn't in the prompts but in the governance: who decides, who checks, and how you know what happened. The third was humbler. A system like this is only as good as the person directing it, and it has made me more deliberate about what I ask for and why.

It is also, honestly, a lot of fun. The site you are reading is built and updated by it.

The departments will change. Some roles will merge, new ones will appear, and HR will let a few go. That is the point. KASCORP isn't a script that does one job. It is an organization, and organizations are meant to grow.
