Summary

Across ten outlooks, opportunity language outweighed risk language 27 to 19, a 42% margin, and 24 sentences used language consistent with an underpricing view of African assets.

The sharper finding is who said it. Private capital read the continent far more bullishly than the multilaterals. Morgan Stanley and BlackRock carry the strongest opportunity signals, while both IMF regional outlooks contain none. That gap says more about mandates and time horizons than about who is right, and it is where the real debate sits.

This is a first pass on a small corpus: 70 tagged signal matches across ten documents, so a single report can move a ranking. The corpus widens each week.

Key findings
  1. 01

    Opportunity outweighs risk

    27 opportunity-coded sentences against 19 risk-coded ones, a stronger lean than the usual headline narrative on Africa suggests.

  2. 02

    Underpricing language is common

    24 sentences use terms such as "attractive", "compelling" or "structural opportunity". That language is consistent with an underpricing view; whether prices agree is the question for Week 6.

  3. 03

    A clear institutional divide

    Private managers lead on opportunity; the IMF outlooks show none. Different mandates, time horizons and risk frameworks, read side by side.

  4. 04

    South Africa dominates mentions

    The most liquid and most researched market on the continent is also the most discussed, which can crowd out the rest of the story.

  5. 05

    FX and governance lead the risks

    Currency volatility and governance carry most of the risk language. Infrastructure gaps and debt sustainability follow. Citi's annual report scores highest on risk.

Method
  1. Extraction. Text pulled from each PDF with pdfplumber.
  2. Cleaning. Disclaimers, page numbers and legal boilerplate removed with regex.
  3. Africa sentences. spaCy kept every sentence naming one of 23 African countries or 9 regional designations.
  4. Scoring. Each sentence matched against three keyword taxonomies built for institutional investment language: 10 opportunity terms, 10 risk terms and 28 mispricing terms.

FinBERT sentence-level sentiment was run alongside the keyword scores; Week 1 reports the keyword scores only. Raw counts are not normalised for report length yet, and the mispricing taxonomy picks up promotional language as well as valuation arguments. Both are being refined as the corpus grows.
